Each square averages his projections against pitchers near that spot, weighted by batters faced. Gray = too few pitchers nearby. Dots are pitchers: hover one for his numbers. The third-axis range keeps only pitchers in that percentile band.

Projected wOBA is the model's prediction for this hitter against this pitcher. It comes from composing per-pitch predictions through the count sequence of a plate appearance. The pitcher is represented by his 2015–2023 pitch mix and locations, weighted by batters faced.

Matchup effect is what's left after taking out "good hitters hit everyone" and "good pitchers get everyone out". It is the projection minus the hitter's own average against that hand, minus the pitcher's average against all hitters (plus the overall mean). In wOBA points: +10 means 0.010 better than expected. It is a small part of the picture: about 13 points of spread, against about 30 for hitter quality and about 45 for pitcher quality.

Pitcher types use within-hand percentiles weighted by batters faced: fastball velocity, fastball height at the plate, and breaking-ball share. A hitter's score against a type is the weighted average of his matchup effects against the pitchers in it.

Hitter profile maps one hitter against two pitcher attributes at once. Each square is a smoothed, batters-faced-weighted average of his projections against the pitchers near that spot (Gaussian kernel, bandwidth 0.35 standard deviations per axis). The four-box table splits both attributes at the median. Colors use one fixed scale for every hitter. Red is good for the hitter and blue is bad, everywhere on this site.

Limits. These are model outputs, not observed results. The model was checked for handedness splits on sealed 2025 data. For finer matchups, one season has too few plate appearances per hitter to confirm or refute them. Pitchers with too few training pitches, and hitters new in 2024, are missing. Switch hitters are shown from the side they bat on against that pitcher's hand.
