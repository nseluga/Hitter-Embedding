\begin{abstract}
Most of what a team wants to know about a hitter is a split: against left-handers, against high velocity, against pitchers who live at the top of the zone. Each one takes hundreds of plate appearances to stabilize, and the decision usually comes before that. The standard answer shrinks the split toward a league or handedness average, which throws away the pitch-level record that produced it. Player embeddings trained on at-bat outcomes or pitch sequences exist, but they are read out for recent form or game-level predictions, not composed into a season split a team would project. We train a hitter model on pitches and query it for the split instead. The model is under one million parameters: a 32-dimension hitter embedding and the pitch context predict swing, then contact, then exit velocity, launch angle, and spray direction as binned distributions. We train on 7.3 million Statcast pitches from 2015 through 2024 covering 1,870 hitters. A query is a weight vector over pitchers. We weight every pitcher by batters faced and compose the per-pitch outputs through the count-state Markov chain, which gives a projected wOBA exactly without simulating plate appearances, and a different split needs only a different weight vector, not a retrained model. Handedness is the first query we validate. On sealed 2025 data, the pre-registered test against gradient-boosted and empirical Bayes baselines did not pass: pooled error was tied and pooled rank order beat both. Measured against the reliability of one season's side-specific wOBA, the model recovers 55\% of the platoon signal and 63\% of the level signal. It beats both baselines only for hitters with 113 to 452 prior plate appearances, and below that 43\% of hitters are unseen in training and the predictions are too spread out rather than misordered. The embedding, trained only on pitch outcomes, separates hitters on power, contact, and discipline at 0.75 to 0.80 held-out accuracy. The same interface answers velocity, fastball location, and pitch-mix queries, but one season is too noisy to score those hitter by hitter, so they are exposed and not yet validated. Code and data: \url{https://github.com/nseluga/hitter-embedding} % TODO: make public before Oct 1
\end{abstract}
