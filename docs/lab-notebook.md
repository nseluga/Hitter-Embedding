## 2026-07-21 — Phase B.1 methodology hardening and results
- **Did:** Hardened the stabilization methodology and generated the B.1 artifacts. Added a variance-components (one-way random-effects) estimator with bootstrap CIs and a sequential-split mode to `stabilization.py` (6 new tests, 70 pass); added `src/analysis/b1_report.py`, writing the panel, common-PA-axis ranking, spray-clipping check, and wOBA survivorship decomposition to `results/phase_b/` (5 CSVs + 3 figures). Logged three decisions. Decided B.1 doesn't warrant its own notebook — the logic lives in the tested module and results/ + this entry are the record; a thin reporting notebook is deferred to paper time.
- **Why:** Two weaknesses in the 07-20 numbers: (1) the single r=0.5 threshold was unsourced, so we now report both r=0.5 and the literature's r=0.7 "reliable" convention; (2) the pooled-process-vs-side-specific-outcome comparison was asymmetric, and split-half at large n only uses hitters who reach that n, biasing the outcome number. The variance-components estimator decomposes signal and noise over all hitters, giving an analytic reliability(n), CIs, and the shrinkage constant; the sequential split gives the across-circumstance number the projection task actually faces.
- **Found:** Survivorship bias was real and material — split-half put wOBA vs LHP at ~435 PA, variance-components on all 2142 hitters put it at ~190. The two estimators agree on a fixed population; restricting to durable regulars halves the between-hitter signal variance (0.00137 → 0.00073) and doubles n*. On a common PA axis the honest process-beats-outcome gap is several-fold, not an order of magnitude: whiff ~28 PA-equiv (~7x), swing ~31, LA ~62, EV ~63 (~3x), spray ~122 (~1.6x) vs wOBA ~190–198. Matched side-specific process stays fast (whiff vs LHP 45, vs RHP 50, ~pooled 51). Spray-clipping barely helps (n* 82 → 73, VC) — spray remains the slowest process signal regardless.
- **Learned:** variance-components / one-way-random-effects reliability and its equivalence to Cronbach's alpha and KR-21; survivorship bias in split-half-at-each-n and how to detect it; the r=0.5 vs r=0.7 threshold distinction and the shrinkage reading of n*; random vs sequential (across-circumstance) split-half.
- **Next:** Run the deferred Phase A `/research-review` before promoting B.1, then start B.2 GBM feature screening (XGBoost + SHAP on the 48 context features, through the frozen split, tuned on val never test). The spray label decision (drop `|spray|>90` vs keep) now has evidence — the gain is small and would require a `labels.py` change plus a parquet regen, so it's Nate's call, not yet made.

## 2026-07-27 — Phase C opens: the claim-1 metric, the C.1 baselines, and a contaminated hitter population
- **Did:** Built the claim-1 evaluation harness (`src/analysis/claim1_eval.py`, 27 gates) — PA-weighted RMSE plus rank correlation, stratified by prior side-specific exposure, with noise-floor deconvolution as a companion. Built the C.1 trailing-average baselines in two variants, raw and bucketed (`src/analysis/c1_trailing.py`, 10 gates). Found and fixed a contaminated hitter population: pitchers taking their own at-bats were in every hitter-talent quantity in the project. Added `primarily_pitchers` / `drop_pitcher_batters` to `eval_targets.py` (4 gates), applied it to B.1, C.1, and the harness, and regenerated the B.1 artifacts. Started C.2 (empirical-Bayes shrinkage constant) but reverted it — see Next. Three decision-log entries.
- **Why:** The harness had to exist before any baseline produced a number — a yardstick authored by someone who already knows whose result it will flatter is not a yardstick. C.1 has two variants because the pair decomposes what a single variant hides: raw→bucketed measures the value of shrinkage at all, bucketed→C.2 the value of doing it properly, C.2→Phase D the value of cross-hitter structure — the thesis. The 3-season trailing window was settled by measurement: it costs the low-exposure stratum almost nothing since those hitters' careers already sit inside it.
- **Found:** (1) Trailing averages are worse than ignoring the hitter entirely below high exposure — skill −30% (low) and −115% (medium) against a league-average reference, turning positive only above ~452 prior PA (+39%). (2) Raw PA-weighted RMSE is 60–70% target noise in every stratum, so real differences read as rounding; the deconvolution's independent check held — the no-info baseline's deconvolved error (.0366) matches B.1's separately-estimated between-hitter talent SD (.0370). (3) 1,223 of 2,487 train-season "batters" were pitchers (median 12 PA, .153 wOBA, within-PA variance .137 against the pooled .261 the code assumed); correcting it moved side-specific wOBA n* from 190/198 to 226/254 and widened the B.1 process-beats-outcome gap to ~7.6x (whiff) and ~2.0x (spray). (4) I'd assumed `clean.py` removed pitchers batting — it removes the mirror image, position players *pitching* — and the eval-target table bypasses `clean.py` by design anyway. Separately, the abandoned C.2 attempt used one pooled within-PA variance per pitcher hand when the real values differ by batter-hand × pitcher-hand cell (LHB vs LHP is .2374, not .261); that wrong assumption drove the whole estimate. Both were caught by Nate's questions, not the gates — a reminder that my estimator gates only tested sample sizes 10–100x larger than the real ones, missing a regime-specific failure.
- **Learned:** noise-floor deconvolution and why RMSE against a noisy target is floor-dominated; that an unbiased estimator can still be unusable (plain method-of-moments returns negative variances when a 2-PA hitter's sampling-variance term exceeds any plausible true variance); the Diebold-Mariano paired loss-differential idea as the powerful form of model-vs-model comparison (deferred, marked in code); that observed variance falling below its own claimed noise floor proves the noise model is wrong — how the pitcher contamination surfaced, and later a second noise-model error too.
- **Next:** Restart C.2 with the carry-forward items from the reverted attempt: within-PA variance must be cell-specific, not pooled by pitcher hand; `scipy.optimize.minimize_scalar(method="bounded")` silently returns its convergence floor when the true variance is ~0, so any variance-component estimator needs a floor guard; gates must cover the actual sample sizes, not just large-n; whether to shrink the split or the rate should be revisited. Also owed: a `c_report.py` following the `b1_report.py` pattern, so Phase C numbers stop coming from ad-hoc commands. The deferred Phase A/B `/research-review` still gates promoting B.1, B.2, and now C.1.

## 2026-07-27 — C.2 rebuilt: the incumbent now beats ignoring the hitter
- **Did:** Built the bivariate empirical-Bayes baseline (`src/analysis/c2_bivariate_eb.py`, 21 gates), added `paired_rmse_difference` to `claim1_eval.py` (discharging the deferred Diebold-Mariano note), and wrote `src/analysis/c_report.py` so every Phase C number comes from one seeded command into `results/phase_c/`. 139 tests pass. All four carry-forwards from the reverted attempt are closed: cell-specific σ², bound guards, gates at real sample sizes, and the split-vs-rate question settled.
- **Why:** Shrinking the two sides jointly rather than shrinking the split is the same model in rotated coordinates, but identifiable where the split is not — disjoint PA on the two sides make the sampling errors independent, so the cross-side covariance estimates the talent covariance with no noise subtraction, where the direct route must subtract a term ~12x larger than the quantity it's trying to leave behind.
- **Found:** (1) C.2 scores +11.8% skill in the low-exposure stratum, the first Phase C baseline to beat ignoring the hitter there (C.1 bucketed: −29.6%); rank correlation 0.022 → 0.169; paired bootstrap CI excludes zero in every stratum, 100% of resamples favoring C.2. (2) Platoon skill exists but its magnitude isn't resolved: SD of personal platoon gaps is 27 pts [14, 37] for LHB and 22 pts [6, 32] for RHB, against average gaps of 30 and 19 — existence is established (0.2%/1.0% of resamples near zero), the factor-of-five range isn't. Switch hitters (98) are genuinely unidentified. (3) The Book's constants look 3–4x too large on Statcast-era data (implied 321 vs 1000, 563 vs 2200) — a lead, not a result, since the ρ intervals contain their values. (4) Every baseline over-predicts, worst in low exposure (+.0164); ~−.0050 is league drift and the rest is managers benching bad hitters. Exposure–talent Spearman +0.24/+0.26. My headline design rationale going in was wrong: I'd sold zero-weak-side borrowing as C.2's main advantage, but only 50 hitters league-wide are in that state; the real win is continuous `n/(n+n*)` shrinkage replacing C.1's step function. The first real run printed ρ = 0.999 for all three types (the clip value) plus a confident split constant of 55,083 derived from it — same failure as the reverted attempt, at a ceiling instead of a floor, caused by `MIN_PAIR_PA = 1` (a 1-PA side mean has ~240x the variance of the signal); a bound guard now exists on both ends. My first unbiasedness gate also shared one seed across noise scales, coupling the realizations and manufacturing fake drift — and let me overclaim in a docstring that the estimator was noise-immune, when only its mean is stable. Estimating ρ bought essentially nothing over The Book's number (statistical dead heat), so the sophistication didn't earn its keep.
- **Learned:** bivariate/multivariate empirical Bayes and the posterior-mean form `μ + Σ(Σ+V)⁻¹(x−μ)`, read as the BLUP so no normality is assumed; that a parameterization can be information-preserving yet ruin identifiability through catastrophic cancellation; that disjoint sampling makes a cross-covariance bias-free where a difference-variance is not; the paired bootstrap as the way to compare two models against a noisy shared target; the Fay-Herriot / small-area-estimation literature as the home of this estimator.
- **Next:** C.3 (XGBoost with context-interaction features), then the Phase C review before any result is promoted. The Option-B amendment to the "read The Book from source" flag is still unwritten (Nate to restate); the over-prediction suggests an exposure-conditional prior mean, legitimate for C.2 but a scope call not yet made; Phase A/B `/research-review` now deferred 4x. Proposed Phase D diagnostic: if process signatures carry platoon information, Phase D should tighten the τ_split interval as a side effect — reportable independently of the claim-1 result.
